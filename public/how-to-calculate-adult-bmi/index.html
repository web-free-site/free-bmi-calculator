How to calculate adult BMI

Medical disclaimer: This page is for general information only. BMI does not diagnose disease. Consult a qualified health professional.

For people 20 years and older, body mass index (BMI) is weight divided by height squared, using metric units or an equivalent imperial formula.

Metric formula

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ [height (m)]². Example: 70 kg and 175 cm → height 1.75 m → BMI = 70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75) ≈ 22.9 (normal range).

Imperial formula

BMI = 703 × weight (lb) ÷ [height (in)]². You can enter total inches or feet plus inches.

WHO adult categories

  • Underweight: BMI < 18.5
  • Normal: 18.5–24.9
  • Overweight: 25–29.9
  • Obesity: BMI ≥ 30

These fixed cutoffs apply to adults only—not to children or adolescents.

Ideal weight range (adults)

This tool estimates a weight range that would correspond to BMI 18.5–24.9 at the entered height: minimum = 18.5 × height(m)², maximum = 24.9 × height(m)². It is not used for children.

Reference: WHO — obesity and overweight (adult BMI cutoffs).
