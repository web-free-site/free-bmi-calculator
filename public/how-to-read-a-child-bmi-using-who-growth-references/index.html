How to read a child BMI using WHO growth references

Medical disclaimer: Child growth assessment in clinical care requires trained professionals. This tool is informational only and is not endorsed by WHO.

For ages 0–19 years, a child’s BMI must be compared to WHO BMI-for-age references that account for sex and age at measurement—not the adult cutoffs (18.5, 25, 30).

Data sources

  • WHO Child Growth Standards (birth to 5 years)
  • WHO growth reference BMI-for-age (5–19 years)

This site uses the open-source child-growth-standards library (MIT) with bundled WHO tables. It is not WHO-certified software.

What the calculator shows

  • Computed BMI from weight and height
  • Z-score and percentile relative to WHO references
  • WHO-based nutritional status label (age- and sex-specific)

Adult BMI categories never apply to children. Ages above 19 years should use adult mode.
